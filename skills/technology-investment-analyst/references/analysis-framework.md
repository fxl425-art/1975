# Analysis framework

## Core sequence

### 1. Thesis and variant perception

Write one falsifiable sentence: because **driver**, the company or sector will produce **outcome** over **horizon**, contrary to **embedded expectation**. List the two or three beliefs that must be true.

### 2. Macro transmission

Use only relevant forces: growth, inflation, rates and liquidity, fiscal or industrial policy, geopolitics, demographics, energy, and technology cycles. Trace each as:

`macro change -> industry mechanism -> company KPI -> cash flow or multiple`

Distinguish structural shifts from cyclical noise.

### 3. Market and value chain

Map customers, users, payers, suppliers, channels, complements, regulators, and profit pools. Size the market bottom-up where possible:

`eligible customers x adoption x usage x realized price`

Test market growth against budgets, deployment capacity, replacement cycles, and constraints.

### 4. Business quality

Assess revenue model, gross margin, retention, sales efficiency, payback, operating leverage, working capital, capital intensity, and reinvestment runway. Identify whether growth is bought, borrowed, subsidized, or organically compounded.

### 5. Product and startup lens

Ask:

- Is the problem urgent, frequent, and expensive?
- Is the product a painkiller, a new capability, or a convenience?
- What is the wedge, and can it expand into a larger platform?
- Why now: technology, regulation, behavior, or cost curve?
- What do users do, not merely say?
- Is distribution repeatable? Who owns the customer relationship?
- Does the team have founder-market fit and unusually fast learning loops?
- What breaks when the company grows tenfold?

For early-stage companies, prioritize product pull, retention cohorts, burn multiple, runway, speed of iteration, and credible learning over polished forecasts.

### 6. Advantage and competition

Evaluate switching costs, network effects, scale economies, data advantages, brand or trust, intellectual property, regulatory position, ecosystem control, and process superiority. State the mechanism, evidence, durability, and likely competitive response for each claimed moat.

### 7. Expectations and scenarios

Infer the expectations embedded in price or financing terms. Build base, bull, and bear cases around decisive drivers such as adoption, price, retention, margin, capital needs, or exit multiple. Assign probabilities only when defensible; otherwise show sensitivities.

### 8. Decision and monitoring

State conclusion, confidence, horizon, catalysts, risks, and signposts. Include a pre-mortem: “If this thesis fails, the most likely reason is…” Define explicit thesis breakers.

## Useful framework selection

- Use a driver tree for economics or KPI diagnosis.
- Use value-chain profit pools for industry structure.
- Use jobs-to-be-done and adoption friction for product demand.
- Use cohort and unit-economics analysis for recurring revenue.
- Use competitive-response analysis for defensibility.
- Use reverse DCF or expectation ranges when price matters more than point estimates.

Do not mechanically include every framework.
