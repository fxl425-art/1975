# Valuation and evidence

## Choose the valuation lens

- Use DCF for businesses with forecastable cash economics.
- Use EV/revenue with growth, gross margin, retention, and burn context for immature recurring-revenue firms.
- Use EV/EBIT, P/E, or free-cash-flow yield for profitable operating companies.
- Use sum-of-the-parts when segments have different economics or comparables.
- Use scenario or milestone valuation for pre-revenue and binary-outcome businesses.
- Use reverse DCF to expose expectations embedded in price.

Never present a target price without the as-of date, share-count basis, net debt or cash treatment, dilution assumptions, horizon, and scenario logic.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. Regulatory filings, audited statements, official statistics, and legal texts
2. Earnings calls, investor materials, product documentation, and direct company data
3. Reputable industry datasets and peer-reviewed research
4. Credible reporting and expert interviews
5. Aggregators, surveys, and secondary commentary

Treat company claims as claims until corroborated. Record source, publication date, period covered, unit, currency, and whether the figure is reported, adjusted, estimated, or inferred.

## Claim labeling

Use explicit labels when ambiguity matters:

- **Fact:** directly supported by a cited source.
- **Estimate:** calculated from stated assumptions.
- **Management claim:** attributed but not independently verified.
- **Consensus:** attributed market expectation with an as-of date.
- **Judgment:** analyst interpretation.

When data is unavailable, say so and specify what proxy is used. Do not convert absence of evidence into evidence of absence.
