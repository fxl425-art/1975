# Report templates

## Source-based analytical article

1. Thesis-led title
2. Short standfirst with stakes and conclusion
3. Opening tension or counterintuitive observation
4. Two to four causal arguments using only relevant analytical lenses
5. Strongest counterargument and its limits
6. Investment or strategic implications
7. Signposts and what would change the view

Do not force report apparatus, ratings, valuation tables, or bullet-heavy summaries into an essay unless the supplied evidence supports them or the user requests them.

## Full public-market or sector report

1. Title with a point of view
2. Executive verdict: thesis, stance, horizon, confidence
3. Investment snapshot: key numbers, catalysts, valuation frame, thesis breakers
4. The non-obvious insight or variant perception
5. Macro and policy transmission
6. Market size, structure, value chain, and profit pools
7. Product, customers, and adoption
8. Business model, unit economics, and financial quality
9. Competition and durability of advantage
10. Management, governance, and capital allocation
11. Valuation and embedded expectations
12. Base, bull, and bear cases
13. Catalysts and monitoring dashboard
14. Risks, counter-thesis, and disconfirming evidence
15. Conclusion: decision and what would change the view
16. Sources, assumptions, and data limitations

## Startup investment memo

1. One-line verdict
2. Problem and why now
3. Product wedge and user evidence
4. Market bottom-up
5. Founders and founder-market fit
6. Distribution and growth loops
7. Retention, unit economics, burn, and runway
8. Competition and prospective moat
9. Tenfold scale constraints
10. Financing, ownership, and plausible outcomes
11. Key unknowns and next diligence questions
12. Recommendation, milestones, and kill criteria

## Rapid brief

- Bottom line
- What changed
- Why it matters
- Numbers that matter
- What expectations imply
- Bull case versus bear case
- Next catalyst and key signpost

## Section-writing pattern

For each material section use:

1. **Conclusion:** one direct sentence.
2. **Evidence:** two to five decisive facts or calculations.
3. **Mechanism:** why those facts cause the expected outcome.
4. **Implication:** effect on growth, margins, cash flow, risk, or valuation.
5. **Uncertainty:** what evidence would overturn the conclusion.
